an idea · not a current opentrade feature
A score you can't train is just a scoreboard.

Those three numbers describe how I decide. XP, streak and coins describe how often I turn up.

counted todayAttendance

Streak, coins, XP, position on twenty-one different boards. All of it goes up if you just keep opening the app.

could be countedDecision quality

Whether the way you think about a market situation is getting better. Nothing currently measures this.

Only one of those is worth getting better at. So here's the idea.

step one
A game is not a skill.

This is the part that usually gets collapsed. One game does not equal one rating. A well-built game measures several abilities at once.

The Decision

Separate the two layers — the thing you play, and the thing it measures — and scores across different games finally become comparable. And far more useful: trainable.

step one · continued
Six abilities, named.

Three experiments produced five of these. The sixth is here because naming the gap is part of the point.

step two
Game → skill → rating.

Every round updates only the skills that round actually tested.

The skill ratings roll up into a single figure, weighted by how much each one has actually been measured — so a skill tested over two hundred rounds counts for more than one tested over three.

step three · the part that matters
Then send people somewhere.

A rating on its own just tells you that you're bad at something. It doesn't give you anywhere to go. That's the gap.

Scoreboard versus curriculum. XP tells you that you played. This tells you that you improved — and hands you the next thing to open.

future concept · not built · not proposed as existing
Market Room

Ten players, the same scenario, the same small pot of virtual capital. Then information starts arriving, in flashes, on a clock.

flash 01The company announces something.
flash 02Analysts react — and disagree with each other.
flash 03The price starts moving before you've decided.
flash 04A second number lands that complicates the first.
flash 05Something nobody modelled.

Enter or wait. How much to commit. Hold or cut. Whether the newest flash actually changes the thesis or just feels like it does.

And the winner is not whoever made the most money — one lucky maximum-size bet is not a good decision. It could score the thing that actually transfers:

returnrisk takentimingconvictionresponse to new informationdecision quality

It doesn't exist — not in this prototype, not anywhere. It's just the direction all of this points.
